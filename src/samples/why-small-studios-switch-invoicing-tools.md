# Why Small Studios Are Switching Invoicing Tools (and What We Learned Chasing Our Own Invoices)

*By Omar Siddiqui, Finance at Brightleaf Labs · Brightleaf blog · 2026-09-10 · 6 min read*

Every studio I know started invoicing the same way: a Word template, a spreadsheet to track who has paid, and a reminder in someone's head. At 3 people that works. At 14 people with retainers, fixed-price projects and milestone billing, it quietly costs you money.

## The hidden cost of "we'll just use a spreadsheet"
In August we had two client invoices overdue at the same time, $9,550 in total. One was 47 days late. Neither client was being difficult: one invoice was stuck in an accounts-payable queue, and the other had lost its contact person. Nobody noticed for weeks, because the spreadsheet only tells you what you remember to check.

Across our clients, the average invoice was paid 41 days after it was sent, while our terms are Net 30. For a studio that runs on a monthly burn of payroll and rent, eleven extra days is a real cash gap.

## What small studios actually need from an invoicing tool
After talking to finance people at six other Lahore studios, I found the same wish list everywhere:

| Need | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Automatic reminders (before, on and after the due date) | Most late payments are forgotten, not disputed |
| Milestone and retainer billing | Fixed-price projects are billed 30/40/30, retainers monthly |
| Multi-currency (USD and PKR) | Export clients pay in dollars, local vendors in rupees |
| Late-fee rules | So the "1.5% per month" clause in your MSA is actually applied |
| An invoice status everyone can see | So the account manager knows before the client call |
| Clean CSV export | So the accountant does not retype totals at quarter end |

## What the tools get wrong
- **Pricing per invoice.** It punishes studios that bill retainers monthly, so you end up with a tool spend that grows faster than revenue.
- **Weak tax handling.** Export-of-services exemptions often need manual workarounds.
- **Reminder emails that sound like debt collectors.** Clients notice the tone, so the tone has to be editable.

## What we are doing
Since September 1 we have been trialling two tools side by side on new invoices only. Every client invoice now gets a polite reminder 7 days before the due date, on the due date, and 7 days after. Our goal is to bring the average days-to-paid under 30 by the end of the year. I will share the numbers, good or bad, in a follow-up post.

## If you remember one thing
Do not choose an invoicing tool by its invoice templates. They all look fine. Choose it by what happens on day 31.
